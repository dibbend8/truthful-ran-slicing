Completed experimental results — 31 October 2026

Training for changed deadlines removes most of Protected's observed welfare
gap. Correcting the payment prevents profitable misreporting under the stated
contract-value model, but does not establish truthfulness for actual delivery.
Higher total welfare can also redistribute service between traffic classes.

The original and new comparisons answer different questions. Originally,
strategic reporting within Unconstrained reduced honest tenants' on-time traffic
by 19.44%. Protected delivered 16.06% higher honest-tenant welfare than
Unconstrained under strategic reporting, with 0.06% lower total welfare under
truthful reporting. The 19.44% measures manipulation harm, not protection's
improvement; the 16.06% and 0.06% compare separately trained controllers. These
numbers remain scoped to the original configuration.

The revised experiment is complete: 108 neural training runs and nine static
weight refits; nine truthful-performance settings and three joint-deadline
reporting settings, each using all 32 test windows. This totals 384 mode/window
cases and 36,864 replayed trajectories (896 x 9 + 9,600 x 3). No setting remains
pending within this revised scope. Reporting tests for isolated deadline changes
were deferred by the time-budget addendum and are not claimed as completed.

All four learned methods train from scratch on 180 training windows for 400 PPO
iterations, retaining inherited hyperparameters. Sixty-six development windows
select among eight checkpoints. Three seeds use the same 32 test windows across
16 reused dates, with 40 seconds of truthful warm-up in each 200-second window.
Traffic, channels, contracts, forecasts and deadline inputs match within each
before/after comparison. The 2,000-replicate date/window confidence intervals
condition on the fitted seeds; these are exploratory, unadjusted comparisons.

All nine 95% confidence intervals for Protected's training gain lie above zero.
For isolated deadline changes, training raises welfare by 2.74–5.30%; its
difference from Direct ranges from -0.023% to +0.684%. With both deadlines
tightened, its deficit to Direct falls from 2.29–3.79% to 0.004–0.104%. It is
then 0.059–0.088% below matched Unconstrained and 1.62–3.23% above refitted static
weights. These findings do not prove equivalence or optimality: Direct maximizes
contract value, not delivered welfare, so exceeding it is not contradictory.

At target load 0.9 with both deadlines tightened, video expiry falls from
38.44% to 26.34%, while background expiry rises from 18.94% to 39.09%. These are
expired shares of resolved bytes, not packet-reliability measures. Target load
is a calibration setting: mean scored-window loads are 0.6854, 0.8823 and 1.0715.
Contracts scale with each load's training demand but remain fixed within matched
comparisons.

Reporting tests cover eight fixed multipliers, each tenant separately, at all
three loads with both deadlines tightened. Protected shows no positive contract
gain beyond numerical uncertainty. On identical Protected quotas at target 0.9,
freezing weights only in pricing permits a 0.94-credit mean contract gain for
Interactive reporting twice its demand. Full pricing changes that report's mean
delivery-utility gain from +1.21 to -5.84 credits/interval, isolating the payment
effect.

Delivery-based deviations still gain in 843 of 6,912 paired comparisons; six
of 72 fixed-strategy means are positive. The largest mean is 0.00482 credits/
interval (95% CI [-0.00228, 0.00976]). A smaller mean, 0.00290 at load 0.7 for
Background reporting 0.9 times demand, has an unadjusted CI just above zero
[0.0000122, 0.00590]. Correlated case maxima are not an implemented adaptive
strategy. Contract truthfulness does not establish delivery truthfulness.

These local queue deadlines do not reproduce complete 3GPP profiles or establish
URLLC/mMTC performance. No new full ns-3/5G-LENA deadline experiment was run;
the original four packet-simulation windows retain limited directional support
and missed the original quantitative agreement targets. The repeated-interval
guarantee retains its stated assumptions, including no effects of earlier
allocations on later service values; queue carryover violates that assumption.
