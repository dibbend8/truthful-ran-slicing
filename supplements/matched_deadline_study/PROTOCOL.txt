Matched deadline training and delivery-utility evaluation — frozen training plan, 31 October 2026

Status
This is a prospective analysis plan for a new, explicitly exploratory extension.
The original and frozen-controller TEST results have already been inspected.
This document is not a retrospective preregistration and cannot turn reused
TEST dates into an unseen holdout. The user has authorized matched training and
evaluation on the research server. This plan is frozen before scientific runs;
each runner records the protocol, exact inputs, code hashes and fixed job grid.

Questions and estimands
1. How much truthful delivery welfare is recovered by training each controller
   for the tighter local deadlines, holding its data, architecture and training
   budget fixed? Compare each retrained controller with its frozen counterpart.
2. After matched training, how does Protected compare with Unconstrained,
   context-only learned priorities, measurement-only learning, static allocation,
   equal weights and Direct contract-value maximization?
3. Does full pricing continue to remove contract-model gain, and can tested
   reports nevertheless profit under actual replayed delivery value minus the
   bill? Report both aggregate fixed-strategy effects and positive individual
   seed/window cases. Do not infer practical strategyproofness from a negative
   average or from a finite tested strategy grid.

Fixed staged scope; reduce the number of cells before reducing scientific budget
Stage A: target load 0.9, both tightened deadlines (video/interactive/background
300/50/300 ms). Four learned arms times three seeds = 12 full training jobs.
Stage B: the same deadline profile at target loads 0.7 and 1.1: 24 additional
jobs, for 36 cumulative jobs.
Stage C: isolated video300 (300/150/300 ms) and interactive50 (2002/50/300 ms)
profiles at all three target loads: 72 additional jobs, 108 cumulative jobs.
Order cells and seeds in a manifest before running them. Stage continuation and
stopping must depend on resource availability or validation failures, not whether
Protected improves, wins a benchmark, or passes an incentive threshold. A report
ending after Stage A answers only the default-load joint-tightening question.
Do not describe an incomplete stage as the planned complete grid.

Runtime planning
The archived default Protected seed-0 record reports 1,978.62 training seconds
for 400 iterations on its original host. This implies roughly 6.6 serial job-
hours for Stage A and 59.4 for all 108 jobs, before preparation, verification,
evaluation and host differences. These are planning figures, not current-host
ETAs. Measure one full Stage-A job and include it in the final grid; use TRAIN-
only smoke tests to choose safe concurrency. Keep one numerical-library thread
per process and record elapsed time, hardware, memory and worker count. Do not
silently shorten all jobs to 50/100 iterations or reduce training windows to
make a deadline. If resources are limited, finish Stage A at the full budget
and clearly label the remaining stages as unexecuted.

Data and pairing
TRAIN: all 180 original windows from 15 dates; 200 intervals/window, the original
40-interval warm-up, and the original composition, channel and reporting rules.
DEV model selection: exactly the original 66 selection-half windows from 11
dates, as returned by registry.window_keys('dev', role='select'). Do not
substitute the full 126-window DEV set. The other 60 DEV estimation-half windows
are not needed for model selection and remain outside the selection operation.
TEST: all 32 fixed windows, two offsets (60 and 43260 s) on each of the 16
original TEST dates. Use the same 32 windows for welfare and strategic utility,
so their actual offered-load distributions agree. Three learned seeds: 0,1,2.
Store exact keys, ordering, array/data hashes and per-window actual offered load.
Target 0.7/0.9/1.1 denotes the original load calibration, not an exact realized
load; report actual mean/range by target and phase. Do not tune composition or
discard dates/windows after examining results.

Shared physical inputs, public service shapes and forecasts
Within each deadline/load cell, frozen and retrained policies see identical
traffic, channels, report bounds, contract values, capacity, g/M/C/mu, measurement
delay, feature definitions, and operator forecasts. The only training treatment
is whether the policy weights were learned for the tighter deadline context.
Apply the target deadline to replay and to every policy's contract/context
features, including frozen policies. Do not compare old-deadline frozen features
against new-deadline retrained features.
Reuse the verified new public burst-shape fit on all 180 TRAIN windows for the
cell, with its exact hash, for frozen and retrained policies, Direct and pricing.
Shapes must not be fit separately by controller or on DEV/TEST outcomes. Neural
PPO still learns from realized delivery welfare; changing public shapes does
not itself mean that the neural policy was retrained. Keep the original fitted
operator forecaster and all non-deadline contract/calibration fields fixed.
Do not silently refit input normalization, means, reservation levels or forecasts
alongside the policy: those would be separate treatments.
Measurement-only substitutes operator forecasts for reports in quota bounds;
all report-aware arms use reports. Thus they share the formula and contracts,
but not numerically identical report/forecast inputs. Kappa0 still uses reports
in the bounds and is a distinct control for report-dependent learned priorities.

Primary training treatment: start from scratch with the original full budget
Train Protected, Unconstrained, measurement-only and Kappa0 from their original
seeded initialization, not from the original fitted checkpoints. This gives
the same training exposure as the original full run and prevents an unlabelled
extra pretraining budget. No trainable parameters are copied between arms.
Retain architecture widths, feature dimensions, precision and algorithm.
For all four arms, the archived inherited selected cell is:
  iterations=400; windows_per_iter=32; M=8 action samples/state;
  PPO passes=4; minibatch=2048; clip=0.2; grad_clip=1;
  learning rate linearly 3e-4 to 3e-5; sigma0=0.6;
  sigma_min=0.05, sigma_max=1.0; entropy_coef=1e-3;
  realized-delivery lookahead horizon=3; evaluation every 50 iterations.
Serialize and verify the complete PPOConfig, rather than relying on defaults.
Use every available TRAIN window as the sampling pool. Persist the per-seed
window-sampling schedule and nominal/actual rollout and update counts; match
them across architectures and deadline cells. Use common seeds for paired
randomness where the implementation permits it, without claiming identical
initial parameter arrays for architectures with different dimensions.
Payments are not a training reward; no learned arm is trained to look good on
the later manipulation test. Model selection is truthful delivery welfare only.

Checkpoint and hyperparameter selection
Run all 400 iterations without performance-based early stopping. Evaluate the
deterministic policy on all 66 DEV-selection windows at iterations
50,100,150,200,250,300,350,400. Select the largest mean delivery welfare; strict
greater-than replacement gives the earliest checkpoint on exact ties. The
actual code provides eight checkpoints, despite stale comments referring to
nine. Do not add iteration zero to only some arms or select the final checkpoint
for one arm while DEV-selecting another. Save all selected and final artifacts,
per-iteration history, all eight DEV scores, and chosen-checkpoint digests.
Verify the saved model reproduces its recorded selection score before TEST.
Use seeds 0,1,2 regardless of their DEV or TEST ranking; never select a best seed.

Hyperparameters are inherited, not retuned per deadline. Describe this as
matched-budget deadline training under inherited hyperparameters. It does not
show the best attainable architecture after a fresh hyperparameter search.
Repeating the historical eight-cell tuning grid would multiply the new learned
job count by eight (864 jobs for the full extension); it is a separate study,
not a hidden requirement or a selectively granted advantage to one arm.
Fine-tuning from the original checkpoint is optional and separate: if pursued,
give every arm the same additional budget and label its extra historical
training exposure. Never choose between cold training and fine-tuning on TEST.

Baselines and their own adaptation
Carry each original learned checkpoint and original static vector into the cell
as explicit frozen baselines. Train all four learned arms for the same cell.
Refit static weights on the same 180 TRAIN windows with that cell's actual
deadline replay, using the original n=3 search grid and weight floor. Preserve
the historical rule: rank candidates by TRAIN welfare; select among the top
72 distinct candidates by DEV-selection welfare, with better TRAIN rank breaking
ties. Record every candidate and both its TRAIN and DEV score. This retains a
strong historical static baseline, but its 72-candidate selection budget differs
from the eight neural checkpoints; do not claim identical optimization/search
budgets across static and neural algorithms. Neural arms are budget matched.
Report frozen static and refitted static separately, not whichever wins TEST.
Equal weights and Direct require no training. Direct uses the same stale rate
estimate and the same new TRAIN-fit shape; it maximizes fractional contract
value before flooring and is not an integer or delivery-welfare upper bound.
Seed-free baselines appear once per window. Do not copy them into three fake
independent seed observations.

Truthful evaluation and main contrasts
Evaluate every frozen/retrained neural arm and the four deterministic reference
variants (frozen static, refitted static, equal, Direct) on all 32 TEST windows.
For Stage A this gives 24 neural plus four deterministic trajectories/window,
896 truthful trajectories. Each frozen/retrained seed pair uses identical data.
Primary contrasts, fixed now:
  retrained minus frozen welfare within each architecture;
  retrained Protected minus each retrained learned baseline;
  retrained Protected minus refitted static, equal, and Direct;
  change in the Protected-minus-Direct gap after training.
Report mean total/per-tenant delivery welfare, on-time Mbit, expired Mbit,
pooled expired/(on-time+expired) fraction and queue boundaries. A smaller
expiry fraction need not imply more welfare because tenant values differ.
Do not infer traffic reliability or application playback performance from a
resolved-byte fraction. Do not claim a retraining benefit solely from the
best seed or from whichever deadline profile improved most.

Actual-delivery profitability evaluation
For frozen and retrained Protected and Unconstrained, test each tenant alone
against truthful opponents at every prespecified multiplier:
  0.5, 0.67, 0.8, 0.9, 1.1, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0.
This is the original eight-point grid and includes the previously observed
1.25 positive-gain case. It is fixed before new model selection and includes
both underreporting and exaggeration; no grid refinement uses new TEST outcomes.
Truthful report=m1 is the separate baseline. Clip reports to the same limits.
All episodes use truthful reports for the full 40-interval warm-up; apply the
constant multiplier from interval 40 onward, preserving identical initial
scored queues. Replays include the entire path and the original finite-window
terminal backlog. Explicitly retain this finite common-history estimand.

Protected is billed with its full envelope price; also bill its exact same
quota/queue trajectory with frozen-priority prices as a payment ablation.
Unconstrained uses frozen-priority pricing. Compute both rules' own truthful
baselines. Do not interpret the payment ablation as a different architecture.
Price all tenants for the truthful baseline and at least the deviator in false-
report runs. Honest harm is measured from on-time delivery/welfare, not absent
utility fields. With eight multipliers, Stage A has 4 controller/state combinations
x3 seeds x(1+3x8) strategies x32 windows = 9,600 strategic trajectories. The
additional Protected frozen bill reuses allocation paths but adds pricing work.
Exact reusable frozen runs may be cached only after verifying code, protocol,
window, shape, policy and initial-state hashes; rerun missing offsets/multipliers.

For each fixed multiplier, calculate deviator gain as its mean actual delivered
value minus payment, less its own corresponding truthful utility. Calculate
contract-utility gain separately using true admitted demand, not the submitted
report. Report honest tenants' individual/total on-time and welfare losses.
Save certified price brackets and propagate both truthful and deviating bounds
to gain bounds. Validate quota equality between pricing and replay; full-price
contract gain must be nonpositive within certified numerical uncertainty.
Forced reports can harm honest tenants even when they are unprofitable; the
contract guarantee is not a guarantee against a malicious loss-taking attacker.

No positive observation may be discarded because the average is negative.
Report, for each frozen/retrained arm and fixed strategy, aggregate absolute
gain with uncertainty, positive-truth-normalized gain with its denominator,
positive seed/window count, and the largest absolute and relative individual
gains with full context. These instance counts/maxima are descriptive and
correlated; do not build a best-per-window report policy from them or present
them as the achieved reward of an adaptive strategy. No tested gain does not
prove truthfulness; a positive delivered-utility gain establishes a counterexample
for that measured setting, not a worst-case magnitude over all deviations.

Aggregation and uncertainty
Use equal date weights. Within each matched date/window, average paired seed
differences before resampling. Bootstrap dates and then windows within sampled
dates using 2,000 replicates and fixed RNG seed 20261031; report descriptive
percentile 95% intervals conditional on the three fitted seeds. Also show each
seed's aggregate result/range. Do not treat seeds, baseline duplicates or the
correlated strategy instances as additional independent dates.
Relative welfare = paired mean welfare difference / baseline mean welfare.
Relative utility = sum of gains / sum of own truthful utility, including only
seed/window pairs whose truthful utility is positive; retain all pairs for
absolute effects and report eligibility counts, fractions and denominators.
Expiry is a ratio of pooled byte volumes. Report bounds from numerical payment
uncertainty separately from confidence intervals from resampled dates.
Prespecified primary contrasts and the complete result grid prevent selective
presentation; reused TEST dates and multiple comparisons remain exploratory.
The old 0.806% result used a sum-of-absolute-truthful-utilities denominator and
different windows/training selection. Matching its multiplier grid alone is not
a direct numerical replication. Do not compare an individual maximum with its
aggregate statistic as if the estimands were the same.

Audit, completion and failure handling
Write a manifest with every TRAIN/DEV/TEST key, cell, arm, seed, training/selection
rule, multiplier, software version and source/data/shape/protocol hash. Separate
frozen, retrained, static-retuned and smoke outputs. Never overwrite prior study
files. Store selected and final checkpoints, history, complete DEV scores,
runtime/worker logs, initial RNG states, and hashes of data and feature caches.
TRAIN-only smoke/engine-equivalence checks are plumbing checks, not shorter
scientific runs. Compare the training reward/evaluation replay against the
verified standalone evaluator before using its selected checkpoint on TEST.
Refuse incomplete grids, mixed source versions, duplicate windows/seeds,
missing payments, nonmatching initial queues, or inconsistent units. Resuming
a partially trained job requires optimizer and RNG state; otherwise restart
that job from its original seed and record the restart, not a partial-history
continuation disguised as a full 400-iteration run. Resource interruptions are
reported, not converted into outcome-based early stopping.
